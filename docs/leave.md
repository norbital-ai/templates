# Leave: one catalogue, generated entitlements, a ledger, requests

This is the source of truth for the implemented leave system, written for the HR controller who
runs it and the template author who changes it.

## The model

| Record               | Job                                                                                                                                                                                                                                             |
| -------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| `leave_types`        | The catalogue: one row per jurisdiction settings version per code. Its eligibility expression, service bands, accrual, year-end and exit settlement, and whether it is the law (`is_statutory`, cited by `authority`). Sealed with its version. |
| `leave_entitlements` | One row per employment, leave type and leave year, generated by the reconciler and never seeded. Sealed with the settlement rules the type stated when it was created.                                                                          |
| `leave_entries`      | Append-only signed movements on one entitlement.                                                                                                                                                                                                |
| `leave_requests`     | Time-off applications only. They never double as balance corrections or carry records.                                                                                                                                                          |

There is no plan version, no statutory profile member for leave, no allocation table, no yearly HR
batch, no provisional carry and no balance column on a person. A balance is a sum of posted
ledger entries:

```text
available(as_of)
  = SUM(leave_entries.days WHERE effective_on <= as_of)
  - held application days
```

Positive entries grant or restore days. Negative entries take, encash, transfer, or expire days.
Corrections append another entry; posted history is never edited.

## The catalogue row is the whole mechanism

A company's leave settings are the `leave_types` of the jurisdiction settings lineage it binds
to (`companies.settings_code`), edited on the Settings app's Leave types tab under the entity
picker while the version is a draft. A statutory row is the law the company operates under:
`is_statutory` with the section of law in `authority`; a company-rule row is the entity's own
policy. Neither is edited once the version is sealed: sealing freezes the whole version and every
row under it, and a change of policy or law is a new version (see `docs/architecture.md`,
"Jurisdiction settings"). Two entities on one lineage share one catalogue; an entity that wants
its own forks the lineage. The reconciler generates each leave year from the version in force on
the year's rule date, and a year no sealed version covers generates nothing.

`eligibility` is one CEL expression over the person, evaluated by the same Reckon engine that
evaluates a pay component formula (`payroll_runs/lib/eligibility.ts`). The context is emitted
whole, with empty strings and zeros rather than absences:

```text
employee.gender  employee.age  employee.citizenship
employment.type  employment.classification  employment.service_months  employment.hire_date
terms.basic_salary  terms.workman  terms.department  terms.payroll_group
children.count  children.under(age)
```

An empty expression is everyone. The seeds carry, for example:

```text
MATERNITY        employee.gender == "FEMALE" && employment.service_months >= 3
CHILDCARE (SG)   children.under(7) >= 1
ANNUAL Part IV   terms.basic_salary <= 2600.0 || (terms.workman && terms.basic_salary <= 4500.0)
```

A malformed expression is refused at write time: it must parse, every `root.member` it names must
exist in the context, and it must evaluate to a boolean. The same evaluator decides which pay
components and claim cap layers apply to a person.

## Who does what

The HR controller does not create entitlements for each employee and does not run a yearly
process. One arithmetic (`lib/leave/reconcile.ts`) does, and it has two ways in:

- **A fact carries its ledger.** An employment, its terms and a child fact each commit with the
  entitlements they imply in the same write: the collection's `before` hook plans the employment
  from the context its batch read and returns the complete set of entitlements and lines nested
  under the employment (`employments/+hooks.ts`; a term or a child restates the employment from its
  own `before` through `api.db.employments.mutate`, a staged write that lands in the fact's commit,
  and the employment keeps a ledger it is handed). A leave request returns the `TAKEN` line it charges nested under
  itself, so the line is held with the request and lands on approval. The hook reads and derives as
  the workspace: a kiosk enrolment lands the same ledger an HR hire does, and the kiosk holds no
  grant on anything the ledger is made of. Nothing a hook derives depends on the day it runs: what
  a fact opens is posted as of the fact's own date (the hire date, the birth date, the terms'
  start), because a held write is replayed through the same hooks on resume.
- **Time's passage is the reconciler's** (`leave_ledger_refresh`): on the first of each month it
  walks every active employment with today's date, posting the months that passed, opening the
  next year, closing years and exits. A catalogue edit starts it for every company on the
  lineage, the seed starts it once, and it can be started by hand for one company. A person's
  facts never start it. The walk is sliced: one run works 25 employments (a test sets `slice` to
  one), touching each as itself so its own hook regenerates the ledger, and hands the rest to a
  new run it starts at once with a `cursor` (company and last employment id), so no run outgrows
  its deadline and a bank-sized seed still finishes.

The authority behind both doors is one rule: the caller is checked on the row it submitted, and
everything a hook reads, returns or writes is the workspace's own work. The kiosk that enrols a
worker holds a grant on `employments` and none on the ledger, and lands the same entitlements an
HR hire does; the reconciler's policy holds a zero-field update on `employments` and the ledger
rows ride that root. Nothing a hook derives depends on the day it runs.

The ledger begins with the lineage's first sealed version. A leave year whose rule date no sealed
version covers generates nothing, and balances from before that version are opening
`MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT` lines, posted and reviewed like any other correction.

The rules, shared by both:

1. For each employment, leave type and the previous, current and next leave year: if the person
   is eligible on the year's rule date (the later of the year start and the hire date), the
   entitlement row exists and its award schedule is posted through today. UPFRONT is one
   `OPENING_ENTITLEMENT`; MONTHLY is one `accrual:<n>` line per month end. A person the type
   excludes has no row at all, and neither does a metered type whose band is zero. Eligibility
   first satisfied mid-year (service months, a child born) opens the row that day: UPFRONT grants
   the band, MONTHLY posts the months left.
2. A catalogue edit that changes an OPEN entitlement's target appends one `ADJUSTMENT` for the
   difference between the new target to date and the awards already posted, keyed
   `adjust:<leave_type.updated_at>`, so a rerun is a no-op and each edit posts once. MONTHLY lines
   after the edit are computed from the new band; the lines already posted stay as they were.
3. Year close, carry lots and their expiry, commute, and exit payout, all keyed by the entitlement.
4. An approved request takes a `TAKEN` line under the id the request's own write nests, so
   whichever write comes first creates it and the other finds it.

Every line has a stable source key, so the arithmetic can run as often as it likes.

An employee application has a separate, single approval stage. Any one L1 Manager, HR Manager, or
Senior Management approver can approve it. The request hook validates the candidate; it is not an
approval layer.

An exceptional balance correction is also append-only. The HR controller enters one
`MANUAL_ADJUSTMENT` with a reason and unique reference; any one HR Manager or Senior Management
approver approves it. The same reference cannot be posted twice on one entitlement, and an
employee cannot post one. This path corrects evidence and is never part of entitlement creation.
Edge cases the catalogue does not automate (a Singapore shared parental allocation tied to a
birth, an over-generous carry an entity wants back) are two ledger lines apiece.

## Year close, carry, cash and leaving early

Entitlements are generated for the previous, current and next leave year. Each is sealed with two
rules copied from the type when the row was created, and both are shown on the balance row:

- `settlement`: what the year does with its unused balance: `FORFEIT`, `CARRY { limit_days,
expiry_months }` or `COMMUTE { pay_basis }`.
- `exit_settlement`: what the balance does when the employment ends: `FORFEIT` or
  `PAY_OUT { pay_basis, misconduct_forfeits }`.

A leave year closes once, on its end date, by the reconciler:

```text
balance at year end = 6 days

CARRY  { limit 5, expiry 3 months }      COMMUTE { ORDINARY_DIV26 }       FORFEIT
  old: CARRY_TRANSFER_OUT   -5             old: COMMUTED  -6                old: EXPIRED  -6
  new: CARRY_FORWARD        +5             LEAVE_PAYOUT: 6 x rate           (nothing owed)
       expires_on = 31 March
  old: EXPIRED              -1
  old: status -> CLOSED                    old: status -> CLOSED            old: status -> CLOSED
```

Every close line is keyed `close:<entitlement>`, so a rerun restates and never doubles. A request
still pending approval holds the close until it is decided. An entitlement receives at most one
carried lot (from its predecessor); on the lot's expiry date whatever of it was not taken lapses
as one `EXPIRED` line, and a later restore appends only the delta.

Leaving early closes every open entitlement on the exit date. With `PAY_OUT` the balance becomes
an `ENCASHED` line and a payout priced at the exit-date terms; `MISCONDUCT` as the employment's
`exit_reason` forfeits it where the statute says so. With `FORFEIT` the balance lapses.

The money is the ledger line itself. Payroll prices each `COMMUTED` and `ENCASHED` line when it
prints it: the days on the line, the statute's basis from the entitlement's rule (`pay_basis`),
and the terms in force on the line's date. Nothing stores an amount. The company's `LEAVE_PAYOUT`
pay component (source `LEAVE_PAYOUT`) prints the total as a base line: a year-end line on the run
whose period its date names under the pay cutoff, an exit line on the final slip, the run whose
salary window covers the exit date.

## Application rules

A request is refused unless:

- its employment, leave type and entitlement are approved and consistent;
- the open entitlement covers the complete date range;
- employment terms and roster codes can measure every selected day;
- the range is after hire, before exit, and outside paid payroll windows;
- it does not overlap an approved or held request;
- it contains chargeable scheduled work time;
- the employee satisfies the leave type's eligibility expression on each day;
- a limited entitlement has enough posted balance after held applications; and
- required evidence is attached.

`UNLIMITED` leave still receives a yearly entitlement and follows the same approval and payroll
rules, but skips only the balance ceiling. It is intended for unmetered categories such as unpaid
leave.

## Payroll and correction

Only approved request rows reach payroll. Paid leave creates no money adjustment. Unpaid leave maps
to its configured deduction component. Every consumed request receives a
`payslip_leave_request_inputs` capture; after capture the request is immutable.

Deleting or changing an uncaptured approved request causes reconciliation to append the exact
`RESTORED` or additional `TAKEN` delta. Entitlement rows and ledger entries themselves remain
immutable. On employment exit, the remaining positive balance is appended as `ENCASHED` when the
sealed exit rule pays out, otherwise as `EXPIRED`; the entitlement then closes.

## Surfaces

- Settings, Leave types tab: the catalogue of the chosen settings version (Code, Name, Statutory,
  Accrual and year end, Entitlement bands, Eligibility, On exit). New and edit use the
  `leave_types` form while the version is a draft. The Contributions, Pay components and
  Holidays tabs beside it are the same version's other rows.
- Leave app: requests of the chosen entity, one live query carrying each request's employment,
  its employee, the entitlement it draws on with its posted entries (the Balance column), and the
  payroll capture that locks it. A request's detail sheet lists the entitlement's ledger read-only.
- Employee self-service, My leave: the balance panel reads `leave_entitlements` open today with
  their entries in one live query; the requests table is its own; held applications are read as
  proposals.
